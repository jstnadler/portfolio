The one spec that earns the price
Sleep Mode | 36-Month Standby

Still Charged in Three Years

Low-power sleep mode holds a usable charge for up to 36 months.

You will use this once every two or three years. It has to work that once, after living in a toolbox through two summers. A pack that quietly went flat is worse than no pack, because you planned around it.

This is the argument for the $316 and it is one row in a spec table on the live page. It is not exclusive — HULKMAN advertises 36 months too — but nobody in the category leads with it, so the messaging space is open.

Answer the objections, not the easy questions

FAQ

Will it start my diesel?

Rated to 6.5-litre diesel and 8.5-litre gasoline engines, at 1,200 amps of starting current.

Will it still be charged if I leave it in the truck?

Low-power sleep mode is rated to hold a usable charge for up to 36 months. Operating range is −4°F to +140°F.

Why does this cost more than a $50 jump pack?

Answer to be written by BendPak. This is the most-asked question on the page and the honest answer involves warranty, support and build standard — none of which I can speak to from outside.

Will it reach trailer tire pressure?

The compressor runs 10–150 PSI with an adjustable auto-shutoff, and includes a nylon-braided hose with a quick-connect chuck.

Can I hand it to someone who isn't a mechanic?

Clamps are keyed and colour-coded to resist reverse-polarity connection, with protection against over-current, overheating, overcharge and short circuit.

WOLFBOX's FAQ answers "will this damage my battery" and openly states their test data is internal. Answering the uncomfortable question is what makes the comfortable answers believable. The price question is left for BendPak because the real answer is theirs to give.
